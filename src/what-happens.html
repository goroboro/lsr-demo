Shared reading, explained

What happens in a shared reading group?

Each week, the volunteer Reader Leader brings a story and a poem to share with everyone. It’s a relaxed hour and a half of reading, listening and talking — no homework, nothing to prepare, and it’s all free.

How a session runs

Each week, the volunteer Reader Leader will bring a story and a poem to share with everyone. These are read aloud either by the leader or by willing members of the group — we never ask anyone to read unless they’d like to. Everyone has a copy of the text so that they can follow it as it is read aloud.

Throughout the session, the group pauses to reflect on the story or poem, working out what’s going on together, sharing their thoughts about it, and perhaps making personal connections to the literature. The Reader Leader encourages discussion, creating a safe space where members feel valued, encouraged and listened to.

Some groups like to work their way through a novel rather than have a short story every week. The inclusion of poetry as an integral part of the model is important: it can evoke strong memories and feelings, and it allows people to get involved who may have a limited memory or attention span.

Each Reader Leader takes great care to ensure that all the stories and poems they use are of good quality, with depth and resonance. They can range from the classics such as Dickens, Wordsworth and Austen, through Seamus Heaney and Elizabeth Bowen, to contemporary writers such as Margaret Atwood and Carol Ann Duffy.

Good for you

Research shows it works

Research carried out by The Reader over twenty years has consistently proven the value of shared reading. In a recent evaluation, 95% of respondents agreed that shared reading lifted their mood, and 97% said they looked forward to the group as an important part of their week.

No pressure

Just listening is fine

There is no need to read aloud or to speak. The literature provides a shared language that can help us to understand ourselves — and others — better, at your own pace.

Free

Nothing to pay, nothing to prepare

Groups are free, open to all, and there is no need to book. Just drop in for as long as you like — once, or every week.

The best way to find out is to come along

Visit a group and experience shared reading for yourself. If it’s a library-based group there’s no need to book — just turn up. For Zoom groups, contact us and we’ll send you the joining link.
